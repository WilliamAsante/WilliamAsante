We mine the rivers of a younger Earth.

Two billion years ago, braided rivers spread gold-bearing gravels across what is now Tarkwa. Pressed into rock, they became the banket, the reef we mine today: safely, responsibly, and alongside the people who live above it.

Who we are

Ghanaian-led. Tarkwa-rooted. Built to last a generation.

Banket Reef Mining is a mid-tier, open-pit gold producer working the Tarkwaian reefs west of Tarkwa town. We started with one pit and a borrowed drill rig in 2011. Today we run two pits, a 4.8 million-tonne-a-year mill, and a team of more than 1,800 people, most of whom grew up within an hour of the site.

From rock to bar

Seven steps. About 36 hours.

Keep scrolling →

  1. 01

    Drill & blast

    Grade-control drilling on every 10 m bench maps the reef before a single charge is loaded.

  2. 02

    Load & haul

    A 40-truck fleet carries ore to the stockpile pad and waste to engineered, contoured dumps.

  3. 03

    Crush

    Jaw and cone crushers break run-of-mine rock down to pieces smaller than 12 mm.

  4. 04

    Grind

    Ball mills grind the ore with water into a fine slurry, 80 percent passing 75 microns.

  5. 05

    Leach

    In carbon-in-leach tanks, dissolved gold bonds to grains of activated carbon.

  6. 06

    Elute & smelt

    Gold is stripped from the carbon, recovered by electrowinning and smelted at 1,200 °C.

  7. 07

    Doré bar

    Bars leave under armed escort for the refinery. Every ounce is weighed, sealed and traced.

260 – 420 m

Banket Reef

Quartz pebbles packed in a dark, gold-bearing matrix. Old river gravel, now rock. This is the ore.

The reef

Gold sits between the pebbles.

Unlike a quartz vein, banket gold was carried and dropped by ancient rivers, settling in the gaps between pebbles. It is spread thin and wide, which is why we mine it in open pits, with grade control on every bench.

The way back up

What goes down must come back. Every pit closes into land the community can use.

Rehabilitation & community

Every pit we open, we plan to close.

Closure starts on the day a pit is designed. Topsoil is stripped and stored, waste dumps are shaped to the natural hills, and finished ground is replanted with native trees, cocoa and food crops chosen with the farmers who will use it.

  • 1,240 haof mined land rehabilitated and handed back
  • 380,000native seedlings raised in our own nursery
  • 100%of process water recycled; zero discharge to the Bonsa catchment

Clean water

38 mechanised boreholes in 21 communities, maintained by trained local water committees.

Scholarships
