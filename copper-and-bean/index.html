    [ How we roast ]

    Ten and a half minutes

    That's a typical roast for our house espresso. Here's what happens in the drum, minute by minute.

    Time0:00
    Bean temp200°C
    PhaseCharge

    1. 0:00 / 200°C

      Charge

      Green coffee drops into a preheated drum. The cold beans pull heat out so fast that the probe falls to about 95°C before the temperature turns around.

    2. 4:30 / 150°C

      Drying

      Moisture cooks off and the beans fade from green to straw yellow. Rush this and the outside scorches before the center catches up.

    3. 8:30 / 196°C

      Browning

      Sugars and amino acids react and the roastery starts to smell like bread and caramel. Most of a coffee's sweetness is built in these four minutes.

    4. 8:30 to 10:30

      First crack

      Steam pressure splits the beans with a pop, like popcorn. From here we pull a sample every few seconds and decide exactly when to stop.

    5. 10:30 / 210°C

      Drop and rest

      The beans pour into the cooling tray and are cold in four minutes. They rest overnight, get bagged by hand and ship the next afternoon.
